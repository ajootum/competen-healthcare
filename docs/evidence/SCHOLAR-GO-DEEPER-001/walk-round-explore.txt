Why reflexes lead and voluntary control follows
Scholar’s explanation

A reflex is a fixed, automatic response wired through lower parts of the nervous system, so it can work before the higher brain areas that direct deliberate movement have matured. Voluntary, coordinated movement depends on those higher centres gaining control, which happens gradually after birth.

As the higher brain matures, it begins to override some early reflexes, which is why certain reflexes fade on a predictable timetable rather than at random.

Reading reflexes and feeding together
From your notes

Because rooting and sucking are nutritive, they connect directly to feeding; smell shows only in the newborn's search for the nipple [S6][S2]. A person caring for the infant can watch feeding behaviour alongside how the infant responds to sound and makes eye contact to build an overall picture [S1][S10].

6. RANDOM MOVEMENT IS NOT DISORDER

Scholar’s explanation

It is tempting to read random, diffuse movement as a problem, because it looks aimless beside a reflex that works smoothly. In fact uncoordinated movement is the expected starting point, and coordination is something the nervous system builds over time rather than something missing.
