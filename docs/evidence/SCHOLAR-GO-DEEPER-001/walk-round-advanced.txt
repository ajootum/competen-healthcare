Why acceptance and resisting appear before learning
Scholar’s explanation

Taste works through receptors on the tongue that respond to chemicals in fluid and send signals to the brainstem, the lower part of the brain that handles basic survival responses.

A newborn has not learned which fluids are good; these responses are built in before experience teaches anything. Accepting sweet and resisting bitter or sour is therefore a protective pattern: sweetness signals energy, while bitter and sour can signal something to avoid.

How smell guides the newborn to the breast
Scholar’s explanation

Smell detects chemicals in the air rather than in fluid. The newborn's search for the nipple works because the scent of breast milk draws the infant toward it.

This means taste and smell act together at feeding: smell helps locate the breast, and taste accepts the sweet milk once it reaches the tongue.

4. "WELL DEVELOPED" DOES NOT MEAN FULLY ADULT

Scholar’s explanation

It is tempting to read "well developed" as meaning taste is complete and finished at birth. What the evidence of acceptance and resisting actually shows is that the pattern of responding is present and working, not that it will never change further.

A newborn responding in this way tells you the pathway is functioning; it does not tell you how taste will mature with age.
