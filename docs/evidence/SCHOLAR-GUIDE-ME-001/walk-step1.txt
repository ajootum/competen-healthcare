How fine motor behaviour and emotion show in the newborn's hands

The newborn starts with limited hand control
From your notes

In fine motor development, the newborn holds the hand in a fist [S4]. When crying, he draws the arms and legs to the body [S4].

Why the hand stays closed
Scholar’s explanation

A newborn's hand stays closed because the muscles that bend the fingers are more active early on than the ones that open them, and the brain pathways that command deliberate opening are still forming. So the fist is not a decision; it is what the immature nervous system produces when left to itself.

Drawing the limbs inward during crying is a whole-body reaction rather than a targeted movement. The infant cannot yet aim an arm at a goal, so distress spreads across the body instead of showing in one hand or one finger.

MEMORY AID: A WAY TO HOLD IT TOGETHER

Think of the newborn hand as a spring held shut: it takes maturing nerve control to release it, and until then any upset pulls the whole body tighter rather than freeing a single part.
