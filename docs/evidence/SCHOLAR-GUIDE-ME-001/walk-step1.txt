Why hearing is tested through a baby's behaviour

The newborn shows hearing through the body
From your notes

The newborn infant usually makes some response to sound from birth, and ordinary sounds are heard well before 10 days of life [S1]. The response to sound is shown through either cry or eye movement, cessation of activity and/or startle reaction [S1].

Why the response must be read from behaviour
Scholar’s explanation

A newborn cannot tell you what it hears, so hearing is inferred from what the body does when a sound arrives. The ear turns sound into nerve signals, and the brainstem can trigger a quick, built-in reaction before any thinking takes place. That is why a change in activity, a blink or a startle counts as evidence the sound was sensed: the pathway from ear to a motor response is working, even though the response is not yet chosen or controlled.

MEMORY AID: A CUE FOR LINKING SOUND TO SIGN

Scholar’s explanation

Picture an alarm: the ear catches it and the body answers automatically, without deciding to. The baby's startle or sudden stillness is that automatic answer to sound.
