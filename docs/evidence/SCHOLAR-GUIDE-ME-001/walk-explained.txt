How the notes connect looking and bonding
From your notes

Within a few days, the newborn infant interacts with caregivers through eye contact and spontaneous or imitative facial gestures [S10]. These interactions strengthen emotional ties with caregivers [S10].

How the notes connect looking and bonding, beyond your notes
Scholar’s explanation

So the behaviour is not just a sign of a bond; it is part of how the bond grows greater.

What the newborn starts with
Scholar’s explanation

At this early stage the newborn infant expresses emotion only through cry for hunger, pain or discomfort sensation. Eye contact and facial gestures therefore add a channel of exchange beyond crying alone.

Why two-way signals deepen attachment
Scholar’s explanation

Attachment grows through back-and-forth exchange. When an infant looks at a caregiver and the caregiver responds by looking back, speaking or making a face, the infant learns that its signals produce a reply.

This loop of signal and response repeats many times a day. Each turn tells the infant that the caregiver is attentive and available, and it tells the caregiver that the infant is reaching toward them.

An infant who does not yet make eye contact or facial gestures gives the caregiver fewer cues to respond to. With fewer cues, the loop runs less often, so the ties have fewer chances to grow.

A COMMON SLIP

Scholar’s explanation

A learner may read this as "a quiet infant cannot bond", which goes too far. The point is about opportunities for exchange, not a judgement on any one infant. Signalling and responding can develop and vary, so a single observation of less eye contact says little on its own; it only means one usual route for building ties is being used less often at that moment.
