Just explain it

What each behaviour is
From your notes

The newborn infant expresses emotion just through cry for hunger, pain or discomfort sensation [S9]. Within a few days, the infant interacts with caregivers through eye contact and spontaneous or imitative facial gestures, and these interactions strengthen emotional ties with caregivers [S10].

Why one-way crying and two-way imitation differ
Scholar’s explanation

A cry for hunger is a signal that points to a need. The caregiver reads it and acts, but the exchange runs mostly one way: the infant signals, the caregiver answers.

An imitative facial gesture is different because it is a reply. When the infant watches a caregiver's face and copies it, each partner is now responding to the other.

That back-and-forth is what builds a relationship. The caregiver sees the infant answering them directly, not just announcing a need, and each turn invites the next one.

What this means in practice
Scholar’s explanation

A cry gets a need met, but it could be met by anyone and tells little about who is caring. An imitated gesture is aimed at a particular person, so it marks the beginning of a shared, personal connection.

This is why eye contact and imitation matter so early: they turn feeding and comfort into moments of mutual attention, which is the ground that emotional ties grow from.

A COMMON SLIP

Scholar’s explanation

Students sometimes read crying as weaker emotion and imitation as stronger emotion. The difference is not how much feeling there is; it is direction. Crying is a one-way call for help, while imitation is a two-way exchange, and it is the exchange that deepens the bond.
